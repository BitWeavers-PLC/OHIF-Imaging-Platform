The screen

Quick
Left panelPatient name and ID at the top, then the current study and its priors. Each tile shows series number, modality, image count and description.
ToolbarGrouped as Navigate, Measure, Image, Layout, Sync and Output, each icon with a short name under it. A small red corner mark means the button opens a menu when you hover it. Tools that don't fit go under More.
Image areaOne or more views. The study opens in a layout that suits it (for example CT axial + coronal, PET/CT side by side, or an MR grid).
PanelsThe buttons at the right of the toolbar, Paint, Outline, Findings and Curves, open a panel beside the images. Press the same button again (or ×) to close it and give the images the full width.
  • To show a series, double-click its tile, or drag it onto a view.
  • View corners show the study date and series (top left), the patient (top right), the pixel value under the mouse with W/L, zoom and Sync: on/off (bottom left), and slice thickness, location and image number (bottom right).
  • View menus sit in a small row at each view's top-right corner: window presets and colour map, orientation, and overlays.
  • Settings is the gear at the bottom left.
  • The workflow menu (top right, e.g. Standard reading) appears when the study fits more than one workflow, such as PET/CT tumor volume or Segmentation.

Quick

Window from area (under •••) sets the window from a box you drag. Manual window/level, in the view's bottom menu, lets you type exact values.

Measurements

  • Length, Angle, Bidirectional (longest diameter and perpendicular width, for RECIST), and Cobb angle.
  • Ellipse, Rectangle, Circle: area, mean, min and max (HU on CT, SUV on PET).
  • Freehand ROI, Spline ROI, and Edge-snap ROI. Edge-snap is for irregular structures: click points around the structure and the outline snaps to the edges between them.
  • Arrow (with a text label) and Pixel value.
  • Calibrate sets the true length of a line, for images that have no pixel spacing (e.g. some scanned films).
  • The rarer tools (Cobb angle, Circle, Freehand, Spline, Edge-snap, Calibrate) are under Measure; it lights up while one of them is in use.
  • Polyline: length along a path (vessel, ureter, catheter). Click each point; double-click (or Esc) to finish.
  • Height difference: the vertical offset between two points as the image is shown (leg length, pelvic tilt).
  • CTR: draw the heart's widest diameter and the chest's inner width, in either order; the second line shows the cardiothoracic ratio.
  • Text: a note on the image. Spine labels: the first click asks the level (e.g. L1), each further click places the next one down; double-click a label to change it and the sequence continues from there.
  • TT-TG (knee): click the medial and lateral posterior condyles, the deepest point of the trochlear groove, then the tibial tuberosity on its own slice. The TT label shows the distance measured along the posterior condylar line.

Hover a measurement to grab and move it. Right-click it to relabel or delete it.

The Findings panel lists every measurement. Copy findings copies them as a table you can paste straight into the report. Download CSV saves them as a spreadsheet.

Image tools

  • Rotate left / right, flip horizontal / vertical, and invert.
  • Magnifier and Reset view (resets zoom, pan, rotation and window).
  • Cine plays the series.
  • Save image saves or copies the view as a picture, with or without the annotations.
  • DICOM tags shows the image header.

Priors and syncing

Know this

Priors are listed under the current study as "Prior · description". Drag a prior series beside the current one, or use the study menu's Compare with current to open a side-by-side layout. When a layout has other arrangements (for example one or two series of each study), a strip above the views shows "Layout 1/2" with arrows; , and . step through them.

Don't miss
  • Series from the same scan link by themselves. Put CT and PET of one PET/CT, or axial T1 and FLAIR, side by side and they scroll, zoom and pan together without F5. A newly dropped series lines up with the view you are reading. Your current view doesn't jump.
  • Priors link where you are looking. Scroll both to the same anatomy, then press F5. They stay together from then on. Press F5 again to unlink, re-align by hand, and press it once more.
  • Auto-align (CT) (Shift+F5) does that alignment for you on CT. Put the current study on a landmark and press it: other CT views jump to the matching anatomy and stay linked. It works even when the two scans cover different ranges. If there's no clear match it says so and moves nothing.
  • Reference lines show where the other views cut the current image. Sync window/level shares windowing between views.

MPR, MIP and 3D

Know this

Hover Layout for a viewport grid and, below it, the reconstructions this series can open: MPR (3 planes), MIP + MPR, 3D + MPR and others. A layout only appears if the series can be built into a volume, so a scout or a set of mixed-orientation images won't offer it.

Don't miss
  • MPR opens at the level you were reading, not at the top of the volume. A CT MIP opens in the angio window.
  • Crosshairs are on as soon as MPR opens. Drag a line to rotate the planes. Drag the centre to move the cross point; at the centre the mouse moves the crosshair instead of scrolling. Drag a line's end handles to thicken it into a slab MIP.
  • Slab projections are in the MIP menu: MIP 10 mm, 20 mm or full volume; MinIP 10 mm for airways; AvgIP 5 mm. Thin slice turns the slab off. While a slab is on, a bar on the view lets you switch MIP / MinIP / AvgIP and drag the thickness.
  • Rotating MIP: the wheel rotates the MIP. Click a hot spot and the MPR views jump to it, which makes it quick to find a lesion in the planes.
  • Dropping a 2D series onto a 3D or MPR view turns that view into a normal 2D view rather than refusing it.
  • 3D: left drag rotates, right drag zooms, the back button pans. Crop 3D (under •••) has coloured handles to cut away the table or overlying bone.

Segmentation

In depth

Segmentation means marking which voxels belong to a structure (a tumour, an organ, a bleed) so the viewer can measure its volume and statistics in 3D. It takes some understanding to get numbers you can trust.

Two kinds of segmentation

You can segment in Standard reading through the Paint and Outline panels (toolbar, right). For a longer job, switch to the Segmentation workflow in the workflow menu, which puts the image views and tools first. Switching workflows closes the current one, and its measurements and segmentations don't carry over. The viewer asks before switching.

A typical session

  1. Pick the series to segment and make it the active view. Use the thinnest reconstructable series for volumes; tools that work in 3D need a series that builds into a volume.
  2. Create it. In the Paint tab, choose New segmentation. It starts with one segment. Add more with New segment (Shift+A), one segment per structure.
  3. Name it now. Use the segment's menu › Rename (e.g. "RUL nodule"). The names go into the export and the CSV.
  4. Paint. Pick the tool that suits the structure (below). Work slice by slice, or paint key slices and fill the gaps.
  5. Check every slice. Scroll through the whole structure with the overlay on. Cut away leaks with the eraser (Shift+E).
  6. Read the result. Hover the segment in the panel for volume and statistics. Use Longest diameters (RECIST) for the long and short axis.
  7. Export if needed: segmentation menu › Save as › Download as CSV report, DICOM SEG or DICOM RTSS.

Paint tools: which to use

Brush and Eraser

Radius is in millimetres (0.5–99.5, default 25), so it means the same size on any series. ] and [ make it bigger and smaller. Circle paints on this slice only. Sphere paints through neighbouring slices too, which is fast but leaks easily. Use Circle near other structures.

Threshold brush

Paints only the pixels within a value range, so you can brush roughly over a structure and only the right tissue is filled. Range uses fixed values (default 50–600, HU on CT). Dynamic takes the range from the tissue under the cursor.

Ranges that work on CT
  • Contrast-enhanced vessels: about 150–600 HU
  • Bone: above about 200 HU
  • Acute blood: about 50–90 HU (check the edges by eye; partial volume blurs them)
  • Lung nodule against aerated lung: about −400 and above

One-click region

Click inside a lesion and the region grows to its edge. It works best on PET. On CT the lesion needs strong contrast with its surroundings (roughly 100 HU or more) and a clear edge. Wait for the + cursor before clicking; ⊘ means it can't segment at that point.

Shape fill and Edit with outline

Shape fill fills a circle, sphere or rectangle in one drag, for rough ROIs. Edit with outline draws a contour that adds to or cuts from the painted region, which is the cleanest way to trim a leak.

Fill between slices

Paint the structure on a few key slices (every 3–5 slices, plus the first and last), then use Fill between slices to interpolate the rest. N scrolls to the slices it filled so you can check them. It needs a series that builds into a volume.

Labelmap Assist and Marker Guided Labelmap

These are AI-assisted tools, available in the Segmentation workflow only. With Marker Guided Labelmap you place include and exclude markers and the model draws the region. The first use downloads a model, so expect a wait. Always check the result slice by slice.

Outline tools

  • Freehand outline, Spline outline (smooth curve through clicked points), and Edge-snap outline (snaps to edges).
  • Sculpt outline pushes an existing border in or out.
  • Each outline tool can interpolate between the slices you drew.
  • Combine outlines (merge, intersect, subtract), Simplify outline and Smooth outline tidy up.

Managing segments

  • Segment row: the eye shows or hides the segment. Its menu has rename, duplicate, delete, change colour and lock. Lock a finished segment so other painting can't change it.
  • Display: fill and outline, outline only or fill only; opacity and border width; whether other segmentations show.
  • Preview edits before creating (in the tool settings) shows each edit as a preview. Enter accepts it and Esc rejects it.
Before you trust a volume
  • Scroll the whole extent. Most errors are a leak into a neighbour or a missed first or last slice.
  • Thick slices overstate volume at the ends of a structure, so segment on thin slices when you can.
  • Show in All Planes makes a snapshot of an outline. If you edit the outline afterwards, run it again.
  • Saving to the PACS is turned off here, because the PACS connection is read-only. Use Download to keep a copy.

PET/CT

In depth

Reading PET/CT in Standard reading

  • A PET/CT study opens as axial CT beside the attenuation-corrected PET, both at mid-body. NAC reconstructions are left out of the layout but stay in the series list.
  • PET in 2D views is inverted (dark uptake on white), as most readers prefer. Press I to flip it.
  • CT and PET of one scan are linked: scrolling, zoom and pan stay matched in millimetres, so the same lesion sits under the cursor in both, even though PET pixels are much larger.
  • PET windows are in the window menu at each view's top-right corner: SUV 0–3, 0–5, 0–7, 0–8, 0–10 and 0–15. When SUV can be calculated the default is SUV 0–5.
  • An Ellipse or Circle on PET gives SUV mean and max.

SUV only means what the header says

SUV is calculated from the PET header: injected dose, injection time, radionuclide half-life, patient weight, acquisition time, decay correction and units. If any of these is missing or wrong, every SUV in the study is wrong too.

Check first

In the PET/CT tumor volume workflow, open the Patient panel (toolbar, right) and check sex, weight (kg), total dose, half-life, injection time and acquisition time against the request or the technologist's sheet. Correct anything wrong and press Reload Data. A missing patient weight gives no warning on screen.

Measuring total metabolic tumor volume

Choose PET/CT tumor volume in the workflow menu. It appears when the study has both CT and PT.

The layout

  • Row 1 is CT, row 2 is PET and row 3 is fusion. Each row has axial, sagittal and coronal views.
  • A full-height rotating MIP sits on the right.
  • All views open at mid-body.
  • Other stages are 2×2, 2×3 and 2×4 fusion layouts.
The mouse is different here

In this workflow left drag is window/level, middle drag pans, right drag zooms and the wheel scrolls. In the MIP, the wheel rotates and a click jumps every view to that point.

  1. Check SUV inputs in Patient Info (above).
  2. Survey the MIP. Rotate it with the wheel, then click each focus to bring it up in all planes. Decide which foci are disease and which are physiological: brain, heart, kidneys, ureters, bladder, bowel, brown fat, muscle, and injection-site or inflammatory uptake.
  3. Create the segmentation. In the Segments panel press New segmentation. It is built on the PET.
  4. Box each lesion. Choose the rectangle ROI threshold tool and drag a box around the lesion on an axial PET or fusion view. Set its first and last slices with the Start and End slice buttons, so the box covers the lesion's whole height and no neighbouring physiological uptake.
  5. Choose the threshold.
    • Max (percentage of max SUV): default 41% of SUVmax inside the box, the usual fixed-percentage method.
    • Range (lower and upper limits): defaults are PET SUV 2.5–100 and CT −1024 to 1024. SUV ≥ 2.5 is the usual absolute threshold. The CT range can exclude air or bone from the mask.
    Use the method your department reports, and keep to it on follow-up scans.
  6. Press Run and check the mask on every slice of the box. Fix leaks with the eraser. Add small lesions with the PET threshold brush (default SUV 2.5–50).
  7. Repeat for each lesion, then read TMTV (mL) in the panel. CSV exports the per-lesion results.
Common pitfalls
  • A box that reaches the bladder, heart or kidney pulls their uptake into the mask, and with Max it also raises SUVmax, which shrinks every lesion's mask.
  • 41% of SUVmax underestimates small or low-uptake lesions. An absolute SUV 2.5 threshold overestimates near high background (liver, marrow after G-CSF).
  • TMTV changes with the threshold method, so compare follow-up scans only when they used the same method.
  • Fusion opacity and colour map are in each view's corner menus (Opacity, Color map). If the fusion looks off, check the CT and PET are the same scan.

Other workflows

Quick
  • Slide viewer: whole-slide microscopy images open here automatically. Draw lines, points, polygons, circles, boxes or freehand shapes to annotate.
  • US lung B-lines: annotate the pleura and B-lines frame by frame on lung ultrasound. It shows the pleura percentage and exports the annotations as JSON.
  • Subtraction (MR): the Subtraction button makes a post-minus-pre contrast series (a new "SUB" series). For a dynamic series, choose which phases to subtract.
  • Time–intensity curves: draw an ellipse, rectangle or circle on a multi-phase series and the Curves panel plots enhancement over time.
  • Reports in the study: structured reports, including CT and PET dose reports, open as a readable document (DLP, CTDIvol, acquisition settings). Drag them onto a view like any series.

Settings and help

Quick
  • Keyboard: search, view and rebind every shortcut. Reset to Defaults restores the RadiAnt-style keys.
  • Mouse: a reference of the mouse bindings.
  • General: language. Changing it reloads the viewer.
  • About: the version number. Include it when you report a problem.

If something fails to load, the message says what happened. "Image data could not be loaded" means the image server didn't send part of the study. The rest of the study stays usable, so try the series again before reporting it.
